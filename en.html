01

How does an account takeover happen?

Account takeover describes the result—someone else gaining control of your account—not one particular attack.

Phishing & impersonation
A link in email, a text message or a messenger may lead to a fake sign-in page. Someone posing as an organization, service or person you know may also trick you into approving access.
Information-stealing malware
Pirated software, cracks, keygens, cheats, modified installers or email attachments may contain malware. Running them can expose passwords, cookies or tokens. Steam also warns about malware disguised as fake game demos or betas and as mod or inventory managers. Some malware can evade antivirus detection or remove itself after doing its job.
Session & login approval abuse
An attacker may steal information that represents an authenticated session, or trick you into approving their QR-code login.
Email & reused passwords
An attacker may control your recovery email to reset passwords, or try a password leaked from another service.

Can this happen with 2FA enabled?

Two-factor authentication still matters. Depending on the service’s security policies and session state, a stolen valid session may allow access without a new login. QR login scams trick you into approving the attacker’s login. 2FA does not clean an infected device.

Opening a link alone does not establish that a device is infected. Distinguish what was downloaded, run, entered or approved. However, a webpage can also exploit vulnerabilities in a browser or other software, so not running a file does not guarantee safety. An account takeover alone also does not establish that the service’s servers were breached.

02

Why can it happen again?

A second takeover does not automatically mean a second malware infection.

These are possible explanations. Login records, security alerts or detection logs are needed to determine which applies.

  • The original infected device steals the new password or session.
  • Existing sessions or app permissions remain, depending on the service.
  • The attacker still controls the recovery email or recovery methods.
  • The same password is used elsewhere.
  • A new phishing, file or QR login attack occurs.
Recover the account + check the device

Removing malware does not retrieve information already stolen. Changing a password does not clean an infected device.

Discord says changing your password invalidates your current account token and logs you out of all devices. Still review email, authorized apps, recovery methods and the device separately. Do not assume every service behaves the same way.

03

What do attackers want?

Attackers may seek items, games or money, commit fraud, or use a stolen account’s credibility to reach more victims. Collecting personal information or reaching other accounts are also possible motives. The actual motive in a specific incident requires separate evidence.

A familiar name and profile
do not guarantee a safe message.

If a friend unexpectedly asks you to test a game, vote, claim free Nitro, scan a login QR code, resolve an “accidental report” or run a program, verify by phone or another messenger. Asking again through the same account may not verify who is in control.

04

Build safer everyday habits

Before you run or approve

  • Verify unexpected requests from friends through another contact method.
  • Open the official app or website directly instead of signing in through message links.
  • Do not mistake a QR login for claiming a reward or verifying your identity.
  • Do not run unfamiliar files, scripts or commands. Be wary of instructions to disable antivirus or add exclusions.
  • Check official distribution sources for games, mods and tools; avoid cracks, cheats and pirated software.

Protect your accounts

  • Use a unique password per account; a password manager can help.
  • Enable 2FA on email and Discord, and enable Steam Guard.
  • Keep recovery methods and backup codes secure.
  • Update your OS, browser, apps and security software.
  • Regularly review unused sessions, devices and authorized apps.
Never hand these over

Passwords, authentication codes, Steam Guard codes, backup codes, login tokens and account recovery links. Do not approve a QR login requested by someone else.

05

If your account is already compromised

Use a trusted device and work through these steps in order.

This is general response guidance. The actions you took and the state of your accounts may change what you need to do.

  1. Stop new sign-ins on the suspect device

    If you ran a suspicious file or command, disconnect it from the internet and do not enter new passwords. Switch to a phone or another PC that is not suspected of infection. Preserve timestamps, alerts, messages and detection logs without exposing secrets.

  2. Secure the recovery email first

    Change its password and end unfamiliar sessions. Review recovery addresses and phone numbers, 2FA, connected apps and app passwords, mail forwarding, filters and rules. If you cannot access email, start with the email provider’s official recovery process.

  3. Review Discord account access

    Change the password; review devices and sessions, email, phone number, 2FA and Authorized Apps. Remove unfamiliar apps and authentication methods, and replace exposed backup codes. Check for messages you did not send.

    Was your Discord email changed? 48-hour recovery link

    Immediately check the change notification sent to your previous email address. Discord says the recovery link is valid for 48 hours and starts with https://discord.com/wasntme/. Verify that the actual hostname is exactly discord.com. Never share the link.

    This process restores email, changes the password and signs out all devices, and also removes phone numbers and MFA methods. Re-add your phone number and 2FA afterward. Discord says support cannot reissue an expired link. If it is missing or fails, use the official compromised-account report.

    Official email recovery guide ↗ · Report a compromised Discord account ↗

  4. Review Steam devices and transactions

    Change your password and use Sign out everywhere under Authorized Devices. Check linked email, Steam Guard, purchases, Market activity, trades and item transfers. If you cannot sign in, open the official support site, choose “Help, I can’t sign in” and follow the stolen-account recovery path.

    Official Steam account recovery ↗

  5. Check other important accounts and damage

    Prioritize accounts that reused the password and important accounts saved or signed in on the suspect device. For financial or payment losses, promptly contact the platform and your payment provider or bank through official channels. If there is financial loss or extortion, report it to your local police cybercrime unit (in Korea, the National Police Agency's ECRM ↗ or the KISA 118 helpline). Warn friends about recent messages using another contact method.

  6. Check the device before using it again

    Scan with updated security software, follow its recommended actions and review what was detected or quarantined. “Zero detections” alone does not establish that no information was stolen or that the device is safe. If cleanup fails or you cannot trust the device, safely back up essential data and consider reinstalling Windows from official installation media.

    A clean installation erases data and apps. Preserve necessary files first, and do not restore suspect installers, scripts or extensions. For work or school devices, contact your IT team.

If you already changed passwords on the suspect device, consider that the new information may have been exposed too. Change them again on another trusted device and recheck sessions, 2FA and recovery methods.

06

How friends and moderators can help

Contain the spread quickly and help the owner recover.

Operational suggestion Adapt this example to your community. It is not a platform-mandated procedure or a guarantee of safety.

  1. Contain the spread. Restrict the suspect account’s permissions; temporarily remove or ban it where needed. For an administrator account, consider revoking elevated permissions first.
  2. Preserve records and remove risky messages. Keep necessary message links and timestamps in a restricted space, then remove harmful links and files. Do not redistribute malicious files or secrets.
  3. Warn members and make separate contact. Describe observed behavior and contact the owner by phone or another messenger.
  4. Review permissions, bots and webhooks. Check roles, recent moderation actions and unfamiliar apps, bots or webhooks. Rotate suspected exposed bot or webhook credentials through official settings.
  5. Restore permissions gradually. Ask the owner to review email, sessions, apps, 2FA and the device. Do not treat one antivirus screenshot as proof of safety.
AutoMod has exemptions

The official FAQ says users with Administrator or Manage Server permissions are always exempt from filter rules. Send alerts to a moderator-only channel and do not rely on automatic filters alone. Server restrictions cannot be assumed to stop all private-DM spread.

Never make passwords, codes, tokens or recovery links a condition of returning. Do not publicly blame or shame the victim. People should be able to report an incident and get help without revealing secrets.
